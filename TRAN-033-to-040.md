## TRAN-033 — Reverse-curve stationing (source concept Q33)

**A — Forward station calculation.** Two reverse circular curves are connected by an 80-ft tangent. Curve 1 has R = 900 ft and central angle 12°; Curve 2 has R = 1,000 ft and central angle 15°. PC1 is Station 10+00. A bridge begins 60 ft beyond PT2 and is 300 ft long. What is the station at the bridge midpoint? Choices: A 16+80.30; B 17+40.30; C 18+00.30; D 19+00.30. **Answer B.** `L1=900π(12)/180=188.50 ft; L2=1000π(15)/180=261.80 ft; midpoint station=1000+188.50+80+261.80+60+150=1740.30 ft=17+40.30.` The tangent is a separate 80-ft length; bridge midpoint is 150 ft from its beginning.

**B — Reverse station calculation.** The PT of a second circular curve is Station 20+00. Its radius is 800 ft and central angle is 18°. Determine its PC station, assuming ordinary continuous stationing. Choices: A 16+48.67; B 17+48.67; C 18+51.33; D 22+51.33. **Answer B.** `L=800π(18)/180=251.33 ft; PC=2000-251.33=1748.67=17+48.67.`

**C — Applied station check.** A roadway has two reverse curves separated by 100 ft of tangent. Starting at PC1 Station 12+00, the first curve has R=650 ft and Δ=20°, and the second has R=850 ft and Δ=12°. Where is PT2? Choices: A 15+04.92; B 16+04.92; C 17+04.92; D 18+04.92. **Answer C.** `1200+650π(20)/180+100+850π(12)/180=1704.92 ft=17+04.92.`

**Tutor transfer:** Recognize arc length versus tangent length; move forwards or backwards through stationing, and include intervening straight segments.

## TRAN-034 — Compound-curve design constraints (source concept Q34)

**A — Maximum radius and minimum arc.** A project-specific layout permits a larger compound-curve radius of no more than twice the adjacent 250-ft radius. For the largest allowed radius, what minimum central angle provides at least 120 ft of arc length? Choices: A 10°; B 12°; C 14°; D 20°. **Answer C.** `Rmax=500 ft; Δ=120(180)/(π500)=13.75°; among whole-degree options, 14° is the least satisfying ≥120 ft.`

**B — Reverse ratio check.** Project criteria limit the ratio of the larger to smaller radius of adjacent compound curves to 2.0. If the smaller radius is 450 ft, what is the largest allowed larger radius? Choices: A 225 ft; B 675 ft; C 900 ft; D 1,100 ft. **Answer C.** `Rlarge≤2(450)=900 ft.` This is a stipulated project criterion, not a representation of a universal standard.

**C — Available curve selection.** A 400-ft-radius circular segment must turn through 15°. Available constructed arc lengths are 90, 100, 110, and 120 ft. Which is the *shortest* available length meeting the geometric arc requirement? Choices: A 90 ft; B 100 ft; C 110 ft; D 120 ft. **Answer C.** `L=RΔrad=400π(15)/180=104.72 ft; smallest available ≥104.72 is 110 ft.`

**Tutor transfer:** A limit on radius ratio and a minimum length are two different constraints; choosing from discrete available designs requires selecting a compliant option, not rounding the calculated result down.

## TRAN-035 — Grade anywhere on an equal-tangent vertical curve (source Q35)

**A — Grade at station.** A 400-ft sag curve begins at PVC Station 20+00 with g1=−1.8% and ends at g2=+2.8%. What is grade at Station 22+40? Choices: A −0.96%; B +0.46%; C +0.96%; D +1.80%. **Answer C.** `x=240; g(x)=−1.8+(2.8−(−1.8))(240/400)=+0.96%.`

**B — Inverse: zero-grade point.** For the same curve, where does grade reach 0%? Choices: A 20+96.52; B 21+56.52; C 22+00.00; D 22+43.48. **Answer B.** `x=400(1.8/4.6)=156.52 ft from PVC; station 21+56.52.`

**C — Applied: verify a proposed station.** A separate 500-ft sag curve changes from −3.0% at its PVC (30+00) to +2.0% at its PVT. Which station has zero tangent grade? Choices: A 32+00; B 32+50; C 33+00; D 34+00. **Answer C.** `x=500(3/5)=300 ft; station 33+00.`

**Tutor transfer:** The grade is a linear function of distance along the parabolic curve; grade at a point is not the PVI tangent grade.

## TRAN-036 — High/low point of a vertical curve (source Q36)

**A — High point.** A 600-ft crest curve begins at PVC Station 40+00 with g1=+4.0% and ends with g2=−2.0%. Where is the high point? Choices: A 42+00; B 43+00; C 44+00; D 46+00. **Answer C.** `x=600(4/6)=400 ft; station 44+00.`

**B — Low point.** A 500-ft sag curve begins at PVC 20+00 with g1=−3.0% and ends with g2=+2.0%. Where is the low point? Choices: A 22+00; B 22+50; C 23+00; D 25+00. **Answer C.** `x=500(3/5)=300 ft; station 23+00.`

**C — Derive PVC first.** An equal-tangent crest curve has PVI Station 50+00, L=800 ft, g1=+2.5%, g2=−1.5%. Where is the high point? Choices: A 48+50; B 50+00; C 51+00; D 54+00. **Answer C.** `PVC=50+00−400=46+00; x=800(2.5/4)=500; high point 51+00.`

**Tutor transfer:** Solve g(x)=0, then convert distance from PVC to station; the high/low point is not generally at the PVI.

## TRAN-037 — Clearance over a crest curve (source Q37)

**A — Minimum sign underside.** An overhead sign is at the PVI station of a symmetric crest curve. PVI elevation is 205.00 ft, grades are +3% and −2%, and curve length is 400 ft. For 17 ft of required clearance above the pavement at that station, what is the minimum elevation of the sign underside? Choices: A 217.50 ft; B 219.50 ft; C 222.00 ft; D 224.50 ft. **Answer B.** `A=5%; E=AL/800=2.50 ft; pavement=205−2.50=202.50; underside=202.50+17=219.50 ft.`

**B — Reverse allowable PVI.** The underside of a fixed overhead structure is at elevation 220.00 ft. A symmetric crest curve has A=3% and L=600 ft, with the structure at the PVI station. To maintain at least 16 ft of vertical clearance, what is the **maximum PVI elevation**? Choices: A 201.75 ft; B 204.00 ft; C 206.25 ft; D 208.25 ft. **Answer C.** `E=3(600)/800=2.25; pavement=PVI−2.25; 220−(PVI−2.25)≥16 → PVI≤206.25 ft.`

**C — Clearance verification.** A symmetric crest curve has PVI elevation 210.00 ft, A=5%, and L=400 ft. The underside of an overhead sign at the PVI station is elevation 225.00 ft. What is actual clearance? Choices: A 12.50 ft; B 15.00 ft; C 17.50 ft; D 20.00 ft. **Answer C.** `Pavement at PVI station=210−2.5=207.5 ft; clearance=225−207.5=17.5 ft.`

**Tutor transfer:** The PVI is on the tangent intersection, not the roadway profile; use external offset E before adding/subtracting vertical clearance.

## TRAN-038 — Bridge/railroad vertical clearance (source Q38)

**A — Working-point elevation.** Rail elevation at the controlling crossing point is 480.00 ft. Minimum required clearance from top of rail to lowest girder underside is 23 ft. Girder depth is 5.00 ft and deck thickness 0.75 ft. Deck top rises 0.30 ft from left abutment working point to the crossing point. What is the minimum working-point elevation? Choices: A 507.85 ft; B 508.45 ft; C 508.75 ft; D 509.05 ft. **Answer B.** `At crossing, deck top≥480+23+5+0.75=508.75; left WP=508.75−0.30=508.45 ft.` Assume the listed deck/girder thicknesses are measured vertically at the same cross section.

**B — Controlling track.** Two tracks under one constant-depth bridge have rail-top elevations 481.00 and 483.00 ft. Required clearance is 22 ft, girder depth 4.50 ft, and deck thickness 0.75 ft. Assume level deck and identical girder undersides at both tracks. What minimum deck-top elevation satisfies both? Choices: A 508.25 ft; B 509.25 ft; C 510.25 ft; D 512.25 ft. **Answer C.** `Higher rail controls: 483+22+4.5+0.75=510.25 ft.`

**C — Reverse clearance.** A bridge deck top is elevation 512.00 ft, deck thickness 0.50 ft, girder depth 4.50 ft, and rail top is elevation 484.00 ft. What is available clearance to the girder underside? Choices: A 18 ft; B 21 ft; C 23 ft; D 28 ft. **Answer C.** `Underside=512−0.5−4.5=507; clearance=507−484=23 ft.`

**Tutor transfer:** Identify the controlling rail/cross section, trace vertical elevations consistently, and distinguish deck top from girder underside.

## TRAN-039 — Recognizing vertical-curve type (source Q39)

**A — Terrain fit.** A proposed profile descends toward a low point in a broad valley and then climbs out, with a smooth transition from negative to positive grade. Which curve type is appropriate? Choices: A crest; B sag; C horizontal spiral; D tangent only. **Answer B.** Grades increase from negative to positive, producing a sag curve.

**B — Applied grade interpretation.** An equal-tangent vertical curve connects +2.0% to −1.0%. What type is it? Choices: A sag; B crest; C reverse horizontal; D no vertical curve can connect these grades. **Answer B.** Grade decreases through the transition; the vertical curve is crest even if its entire profile does not have an internal high point under different grade combinations.

**C — Misleading visual cue.** A roadway enters a cut section, changing from −2.0% to +1.0% grade. Which statement correctly identifies the vertical alignment? Choices: A It must be crest because the roadway is in cut; B It is sag because algebraic grade increases; C It is a horizontal spiral; D Cut/fill alone determines curve type. **Answer B.** Curve type is determined by change in grade; earthwork context alone does not determine it.

**Tutor transfer:** Classify by signed grades, not by whether the site is in cut or fill.

## TRAN-040 — Crest-curve K-value / SSD length (source Q40)

**A — Minimum length from stipulated K.** A project-specific design table assigns a minimum crest-curve K of 84 ft/percent for the selected sight-distance criterion. Proposed grades are +1.4% and −1.8%. What minimum curve length satisfies this criterion? Choices: A 168.0 ft; B 268.8 ft; C 320.0 ft; D 384.0 ft. **Answer B.** `A=|−1.8−1.4|=3.2%; L=KA=84(3.2)=268.8 ft.`

**B — Reverse K calculation.** A 600-ft crest vertical curve changes grade from +3.0% to −1.0%. What is its K value? Choices: A 100; B 125; C 150; D 200 ft/percent. **Answer C.** `A=4%; K=L/A=600/4=150 ft/percent.`

**C — General sight-distance formula with a domain check.** For a crest curve with SSD S=425 ft, grade difference A=4% (percentage points), driver eye height h1=3.5 ft, and object height h2=2.0 ft, determine the minimum geometric curve length for the specified SSD. Use `L=A S²/[200(√h1+√h2)²]` when S≤L; otherwise use `L=2S−200(√h1+√h2)²/A`. Choices: A 210 ft; B 260 ft; C 310 ft; D 425 ft. **Answer C.** Testing the S≤L equation gives `L=334.75 ft`, inconsistent with S≤L because `425>334.75`. Use S>L: `L=2(425)−200(√3.5+√2)²/4=310.45 ft≈310 ft`. Check `425>310.45`, so the correct equation was used. The assumed eye/object heights are stated inputs, not general reference defaults.

**Tutor transfer:** K is length per percent grade change; an assumed sight-distance equation must be checked against its domain after solving.
